02 — Where Your Input Is Needed

Five decisions only you can make.

These items are blocked or partially blocked waiting on decisions, content, or scheduling that requires your direction. Everything else can move without you.

Decision 01

Logo Wall Material

The dimensional mark for the NE wall needs a material call: brass, blackened steel, or stained wood. Each carries a different tone. Vendor quote is pending on all three — once you pick a direction, fabrication can begin.

This sets the physical tone for every client and agent who walks through the door. The material says as much as the mark itself.
Decision 02

Hallway Print Content

Two 24×36" acrylic panels need graphic content selected. Options: aerial/neighborhood photography, brand typography compositions, or the Savile Row reference imagery from the brand story.

The hallway is the first thing visitors see — it bridges the entryway and the closing room. The content should reinforce place and presence.
Decision 03

Photo Shoot Scheduling

The professional photo shoot feeds every other recommendation in the listing refresh: GBP, Yelp, website team bios, social content. A 2–3 hour session at the C Street office. Budget: $800–1,500.

Until this happens, the listing refresh strategy is a plan without assets. It’s the single dependency that unblocks the most downstream work.
Decision 04

Team Bios & Headshots

Who should appear on the site? Names, titles, bar admissions, and a 2-sentence bio per person. Headshots come from the photo shoot (Decision 03), but the content can be drafted now.

Competitors like KVS Title prominently showcase their attorneys. This is the biggest gap in Woodland’s “attorney-led” differentiator.
Decision 05

Website Review & Launch

The refreshed site is built and ready for review. All pages are live in the preview environment. Needs a walkthrough, any content corrections, and a launch date.

The site was started at your direction so the launch wouldn’t slip while Phase 2 install timing was being arranged. It’s ready when you are.
03 — Design Principles in Play

The thread that connects everything.

Six design principles guiding every decision across the engagement — from the logo wall to the meta description.

🏪

Savile Row, Not Silicon Valley

The brand is modeled on centuries-old London tailoring shops: present but not pushy, building reputation through everyday hard work. No loud marketing, no billboards — just showing up and doing excellent work.

🌊

Be the Undercurrent

Title sits in a unique position: critical to the deal, but when done right, nearly invisible. The design should feel like the underlying current, not the wave. Steady. Assured. Unhurried.

📜

Editorial Warmth

The visual language pairs serif elegance (EB Garamond) with sans-serif clarity (Inter). Warm neutrals, generous whitespace, and italic display headings in sage create a tone that’s professional without being cold.

  • Warm color temperature throughout (no cool grays)
  • Serif for editorial moments, sans for working type
  • Sage green reserved for decorative display headings
🏢

Grounded in Place

Capitol Hill, Eastern Market, the C Street office. The brand is physically rooted in a specific place. Design decisions reinforce local presence — from the GBP photos to the neighborhood landing pages.

👥

Show, Don’t Tell

Demonstrate reliability through actions: the buyer intro call, timely documents, minute-not-hour response times. The design embodies this by letting proof points and reviews speak louder than claims.

🛠

Breadth as a Feature

Residential, commercial, agricultural — DC, Maryland, Virginia. The range is deliberate and unusual for a firm this size. The design communicates this through service pages that don’t silo the practice.

Smaller calls, still worth making.

These aren’t blockers, but they’re decisions that would sharpen the output if addressed. They can happen anytime.
