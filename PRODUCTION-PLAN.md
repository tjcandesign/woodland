## 1 · Business cards

**Brand.** Woodland (existing) + Bainbridge (new set).
**Priority.** High.
**Format.** Two distinct sets, not a flip-side combo card.

### Specs
- Trim size: 3.5" × 2" (US standard).
- Stock: thick uncoated cover, target 130–160 lb. Same paper across both brands so they read as a family.
- Finish: matte uncoated by default. Optional letterpress strike for the mark, brand-specific (gold for Woodland, blind deboss or dark blue for Bainbridge).
- Color way:
  - **Woodland:** cream paper with navy + gold ink, sage italic for editorial type, brown for fine print.
  - **Bainbridge:** stock to be determined alongside the Bainbridge identity work; same paper weight and uncoated finish to keep the family.
- Mark placement: Woodland circle monogram on the back; Bainbridge equivalent mark on the back of its own card.
- Information set (per card): name, title, bar admissions where applicable, direct line, mobile (optional), email, office address, firm URL.

### Notes
- Bainbridge gets its own card so the two brands can hand them out independently. Sharing paper/finish is the unifier.
- Print as a pair from the same vendor in the same job to keep paper batch and ink calibration consistent.

### Open questions
- Final headcount per brand × cards per person (drives quantity).
- Are we including a QR to a vCard or to woodlandtitledc.com / bainbridge-law.vercel.app? Recommendation: skip QR; keep the card editorial.
- Bainbridge typographic system — is it identical to Woodland or does Bainbridge get its own subtle variant (different italic weight, different gold)?

### Dependencies
- Bainbridge identity decisions on color and mark must be locked first.
- Final name/title list with email + phone for each cardholder.

### Recommended next action
Confirm the Bainbridge identity system (mark, palette, italic treatment), then have both card sets designed in one pass and quoted by Mama's Sauce or a similar letterpress shop alongside a digital-print backup quote (e.g., Moo cotton stock).

## 2 · Letterhead and digital document templates

**Brand.** Woodland.
**Priority.** High — daily use through Qualia.

### Specs
- **Printed letterhead** — US Letter (8.5" × 11"), 32 lb writing or 70 lb text, uncoated cream or warm white, single-sided. Logo top-left at ~1.25" tall, address block top-right or in footer, fine rule above the footer in tan. Margins: 1" sides, 0.875" top, 1" bottom.
- **Word template (.docx) for Qualia** — same logo placement, same address block, same footer rule, identical type stack rendered in available system fonts (EB Garamond → Cambria fallback; Inter → Calibri fallback). Header and footer set up as native Word header/footer so the team can Copy → Paste Special into existing Qualia templates without breaking pagination.
- **PDF master** — exported from the .docx as the canonical export reference, plus a separate press-ready PDF of the printed sheet.
- **Continuation sheet** — a second .docx variant with a smaller header (logo only, no address) for pages 2+.

### Notes
- The Word file is the priority. Qualia accepts user-uploaded headers; the team will paste this header into each existing Qualia document so closing letters, payoff statements, etc. all carry the brand without us touching Qualia internals.
- Printed and digital must look identical at a glance. Same logo position, same fine rule, same address block.
- Build the .docx using Word's native styles (Heading 1, Body, Footer) rather than text boxes so it survives editing.

### Open questions
- Do we want a watermark or "DRAFT" stamp variant for unsigned working documents?
- Should the address block include the email + phone, or only the postal address + URL?
- Fonts in Word — install EB Garamond + Inter on every team machine, or default to Cambria/Calibri and let print masters carry the brand fonts?

### Dependencies
- Final office address + phone + URL string (already on the website but worth confirming for the printable masters).
- Decision on font installation across the team (IT effort if we go with brand fonts).

### Recommended next action
Design the printed letterhead first, then back-port to .docx. Have one team member install EB Garamond and Inter and test the .docx in Qualia end-to-end before we declare it shippable.

## 3 · Thank-you notes / stationery

**Brand.** Woodland primary; Bainbridge optional second set.
**Priority.** Medium.
**Format.** Folded note card with matching envelope, sold as boxed sets.

### Specs
- Card trim: 4.25" × 5.5" folded (A2 size). Industry standard, matches stock envelopes.
- Stock: same uncoated cover as business cards, 110–130 lb. Cream or warm white, no shimmer.
- Front: small Woodland mark, blind-deboss or letterpress in gold, centered. No words on the front.
- Inside: blank. Let the writer write.
- Back of envelope: address block printed in tan ink, small mark on the flap.
- Quantity: start at 250 cards + 250 envelopes. Reorder as needed.

### Notes
- The brief is "refined, warm, legal-establishment, high-touch." Avoid anything gimmicky — no foiled patterns, no "Thanks!" pre-printed inside, no quirky illustration.
- A handwritten note in a quiet, beautifully made card is the entire point. The card is a vehicle, not the message.
- If Bainbridge gets its own set, mirror the spec exactly — same trim, same stock, different mark.

### Open questions
- Pre-printed return address on the envelope, or stamped/applied separately so addresses can change?
- Letterpress (more premium, longer lead) vs flat digital print on uncoated stock (faster, cheaper, still good)?
- Single-card box or box-of-25?

### Dependencies
- Same paper choice as business cards — ideally batched as one paper purchase.

### Recommended next action
Quote a 250/250 letterpress run alongside a 250/250 digital alternative; show the team both samples on the same paper so the price/feel tradeoff is concrete.

## 4 · Branded notepads

**Brand.** Woodland primary; consider Bainbridge variant.
**Priority.** Medium. Daily use; client-facing in meetings.

### Specs
- Pad size: **4.25" × 8.25"** (the size the client likes).
- Sheets per pad: 50 sheets.
- Binding: glue-top tear-off (padded at the top edge).
- Backer: chipboard backing, undyed or kraft.
- Stock: 60 lb uncoated text, warm white. Optional: faint horizontal rules in tan, or fully unruled (designer's preference recommended unruled — keeps it editorial).
- Printing: one-color top header with the Woodland logotype + a thin gold rule below. Footer optional with the URL and city in small caps.
- Quantity: start at 100 pads (≈ 5,000 sheets total) for a first run.

### Notes
- This is a quiet daily-use object. Restraint matters — the brand should feel inevitable on the page, not loud.
- Long format (taller than wide) makes it feel more lawyer-grade than steno-style.
- Whether Bainbridge gets its own pad: defer to whether the team uses Bainbridge in meetings vs back-office. If Bainbridge meets clients separately, give it its own.

### Open questions
- Ruled vs unruled — recommendation: unruled, but worth showing one of each.
- One pad per attorney + a stack at reception, or larger first run for full team distribution?
- Print method — letterpress header (most refined, costlier per pad) or flat digital top print (fine for daily use, cheaper at this volume).

### Dependencies
- Logotype + rule treatment finalized as a stand-alone asset.

### Recommended next action
Quote 100 pads in two variants from a pad-printing vendor (Mama's Sauce, Vistaprint pro, or a local Capitol Hill print shop). Get one Woodland-only sample printed before committing to a larger run.

## 5 · Email signatures

**Brand.** Woodland and Bainbridge.
**Priority.** High — touched by every outbound email.
**Format.** Two variants per brand: text-only and lightweight image-mark.

### Specs

**Text-only variant (default for replies and long threads):**
```
Name, Title
Woodland Estate & Title  ·  woodlandtitledc.com
202.516.6855  ·  name@woodlandtitleDC.com
659 C Street, SE  ·  Washington, DC 20003
```
- Single typeface: client-default sans (Calibri or Aptos). Plain. No image, no HTML colors beyond a single sage-dark name accent if it renders consistently.
- A horizontal rule above the fraud-warning footer.

**Image-mark variant (default for first-touch external):**
- Same text block.
- Above the text, a small Woodland circle monogram, 64 × 64 px at 1×, 128 × 128 at 2×.
- Hosted as a stable URL on the marketing domain (e.g. `woodlandtitledc.com/assets/email-mark-woodland.png`) so the image isn't an attachment.
- ALT text: "Woodland Estate & Title."

**Bainbridge variant:** same structure with the Bainbridge mark and contact details.

**Fraud warning footer (preserved):**
- Keep whatever legal/fraud language is currently in use.
- Set in 9 px gray, single block, separated from the signature by a hairline.

### Notes
- The circular monogram works because it reads as a notarial mark. Keep it small. Resist the urge to add a banner image, an ad, or a "Schedule a meeting" button — every one of those creates attachment clutter and visually downgrades the signature.
- Hosting the mark via stable URL avoids the "Outlook attaches the inline image to every reply" problem.
- Build the signature in plain HTML so Outlook + Apple Mail + Gmail all render it the same. No background colors, no table-based decorations beyond what's needed for layout.
- Provide clear install instructions per client (Outlook .htm import, Gmail "create signature" paste, Apple Mail signature).

### Open questions
- Does the firm want one signature per person or a centrally managed signature service (e.g., Exclaimer, Mailtastic)? Recommendation: skip the service for now; central management is real overhead.
- Should personal mobile numbers appear? Default: no, unless the individual asks for it.
- Reply signatures — do we want a stripped, name-only version for the 4th+ message in a thread? Recommendation: yes; ship a "thread reply" variant with just `Name · Woodland Estate & Title`.

### Dependencies
- Mark hosted at a stable URL (we already have `assets/circle-dark.svg` and `circle-white.svg` in the codebase — produce a 128px PNG and 256px PNG export and host them at `/assets/email-mark-woodland.png` and a Bainbridge equivalent).
- Final fraud-warning copy from the firm.

### Recommended next action
Draft three signature blocks per brand (first-touch, default, thread-reply), preview in Outlook + Gmail + Apple Mail screenshots, send to the team for sign-off, then ship installation instructions.

## 6 · Office signing-room signage

**Brand.** Woodland.
**Priority.** Medium. Vendor-dependent.
**Concept.** Large dimensional Woodland signage installed on the brick wall between the two signing chairs — the visual centerpiece of the signing room and the photo moment that ends every closing.

### Specs
- Width: ≈ 4 feet. Height proportional to the chosen treatment (likely 14–18" for a horizontal lockup).
- Construction: individual dimensional letters, not a single backplate. Cleaner shadow, more architectural.
- Material options:
  - **Unvarnished brass** — preferred direction. Will patina warmly over time.
  - **Dark blue powder-coated steel** — tighter, more graphic; good fallback if brass quotes too high.
  - **Brushed bronze** as a third option (warmer than brass at install, less patina drift).
- Mount: stainless studs into the brick mortar lines, 3/8" stand-off depth so each letter floats. Concealed studs only — no visible hardware.
- Lighting: external picture lights or a single linear LED bar mounted above, casting down. **Skip backlit halo letters** — the brick + power-routing constraint makes that a mess.
  - Recommended fixture: 24"–36" picture light above the wordmark, hardwired or hardwired-look battery (House of Antique Hardware or a similar fixture).
  - Wire run: through the mortar joint or fished from above behind the wall, not surface-conduit.
- Mounting tape (3M VHB) is acceptable for the picture light if the brick face is too irregular for hardwired mounting; the letters themselves stay pinned through the brick.

### Notes
- The wall is brick. Every detail of the install is bricks-vs-cleanliness. Pre-survey the wall before committing to a fabrication spec.
- The sign should read at distance and look intentional in a tight signing-room photo.
- One sign, one statement. Don't add a tagline or address line beneath.

### Open questions
- Brass weight — solid brass letters ($$$) vs brass-faced aluminum letters ($$) — visually similar at 4 ft viewing distance.
- Lighting style — integrated linear LED (modern) vs traditional brass picture light (editorial). Recommendation: traditional picture light. It matches the brand's editorial register.
- Power source — if hardwiring isn't available, plug-in fixtures are acceptable as long as the cord runs cleanly down the wall behind a paintable cover or is concealed in a junction routed through the floor.

### Dependencies
- Wall survey: brick condition, stud locations, available power.
- Final wordmark approval (full Woodland Estate & Title vs short Woodland) and exact letter spacing.
- Vendor selection — recommend Gemini Inc. or a regional dimensional-letter shop with brass experience. Local DC-area: ASI Sign Studio or Brilliant Sign Studio.

### Recommended next action
Survey the wall in person. Photograph and measure the proposed install zone. Get fabrication quotes from two vendors (one national, one local) for both brass and dark-blue options, plus a separate quote from an electrician for the picture light. Don't fabricate until lighting is decided.

## 7 · Awards / recognition displays

**Brand.** Woodland.
**Priority.** Medium.
**Concept.** Acrylic standoff-mounted display set, sized to fit between the office's narrow window pillars, reading as a curated wall of recognition rather than a trophy shelf.

### Specs
- Pieces: one per recognition. Plan for ≥ 3 to allow for growth.
- Width: ≤ 28" available between pillars. Target each piece at **22–24" wide × 28–32" tall** to leave 2–3" of breathing room on either side.
- Construction: 1/4" cast acrylic, second-surface print, edge-polished, four chrome standoffs at the corners, ~3/4" offset from wall. Same construction as the Top 40 piece in the Phase 2 install plan.
- Print content per piece: the original article or recognition reproduced cleanly, plus a small "Woodland Estate & Title" lockup at the bottom in tan.
- Suggested set:
  1. Washingtonian recognition
  2. Fidelity Top 10
  3. Stewart top-producer or other excellence award
- Spacing: 4–6" between pieces if more than one fits between a single pillar pair; otherwise distribute one per pillar bay.

### Notes
- Reuse the same acrylic vendor as the Phase 2 hallway pair to keep the construction consistent across the office.
- Frame the wall as a quiet permanent record. Avoid hanging anything that lacks editorial caliber — if a recognition is a generic certificate, redesign it for the wall before printing.
- The acrylic price calculator on `/work` already handles the math for these pieces. At $0.45/sq in placeholder pricing, a 22×30 piece is ≈ $297 each.

### Open questions
- Source files for each recognition — do we have publication-quality scans of the Washingtonian feature and Fidelity Top 10 acknowledgment, or do we need to recreate the layouts on-brand?
- Are we displaying the recognitions verbatim (publication branding intact) or redesigning each into a Woodland-branded "as recognized by..." plaque?
- Lighting — match the signing-room picture light treatment, or leave the wall ambient?

### Dependencies
- Final recognition list and high-res source files.
- Pillar measurements confirmed in person.
- Acrylic vendor (same as Phase 2).

### Recommended next action
Do a first pass with **two** pieces (Washingtonian + Fidelity Top 10), printed and installed. Reserve the third bay for a future addition rather than overcrowding the wall on day one.

## 8 · Stair / entry experience

**Brand.** Woodland.
**Priority.** Medium.
**Concept.** A long, story-led wall treatment along the stair wall — archival imagery treated as placemaking, not as decoration.

### Specs
- Wall under treatment: the **left wall** going up/down the stairs.
- Format options:
  1. **Single horizontal long-format print** — ≈ 24" tall × 60–84" wide. One piece, dominant.
  2. **2' × 4' vertical** at the landing — single piece, framed, anchoring the front room.
  3. **Triptych** — three smaller framed pieces (e.g., 16" × 24" each) in a horizontal sequence telling the origin story.
- Materials:
  - Archival pigment print on cotton rag, mounted to dibond, framed in a thin oak or walnut shadow frame, no glass.
  - Or acrylic standoff print like the hallway pair, if matching that family is preferred.
- Imagery direction:
  - Historical Eastern Market exterior (already in the brand asset library — `eastern-market.jpeg`).
  - Historical Lancaster County image — needs sourcing (origin story for the firm).
  - Capitol Hill engraved map crop — already in the asset library — possibly as a runner if we go horizontal.
  - Subtle, monochrome-leaning. No people-in-suits stock photos. No flat illustration.

### Notes
- The brief is "understated and story-driven, not overly literal." That rules out anything that reads as a brand banner; lean toward an image that could plausibly hang in a private library.
- The horizontal runner option is the most distinctive of the three. It reads as architecture, not as art.
- A very long single image (e.g., 30" × 90") frames the stair as a procession. Higher install effort, higher payoff.

### Open questions
- Direction: single dominant image, vs landing piece, vs triptych. Recommendation: start with one dominant horizontal piece; add elsewhere later if the wall calls for more.
- Lancaster County image — what source? A historical society or public-domain archive scan, or a commissioned new photograph?
- Frame vs frameless — frameless dibond is more contemporary; thin oak is more editorial. Recommendation: thin oak shadow frame, no glass.

### Dependencies
- Wall measurements and stairway sightline photos from the actual install location.
- Final image sourced and licensed (especially the Lancaster County reference, which may need historical society permission).
- Archival print vendor (Image Source Photo Lab in DC, or Bay Photo for cotton rag mounting).

### Recommended next action
Walk the stairwell, photograph the wall at the angle a visitor first sees it, sketch the three options at scale on the photo, and bring those mockups to the next review. Decide direction before sourcing the Lancaster image.

## 9 · Public profile refresh — later phase

**Brand.** Woodland and Bainbridge. Treat as Phase 4 work, separate from the production above.

### Checklist
- **Google Business Profile**
  - Confirm category set to "Title Company" (not "Real Estate Agent" or generic).
  - Replace all profile photos with the new photography pass.
  - Add the team photo set (when shot).
  - Set hours, services, and Q&A entries (seed 6–10 questions/answers).
  - Refresh the profile description with the canonical positioning copy.
  - Bainbridge — claim and stand up its own GBP.
- **Yelp**
  - Claim and refresh the Woodland listing.
  - Replace photos and confirm hours/services.
  - Bainbridge — stand up if the firm wants visibility there; otherwise skip.
- **LinkedIn**
  - Personal: refresh CJ's profile photo, banner, About section, and current role copy. Link to the firm.
  - Business: refresh Woodland Estate & Title company page with new logo, banner, About section, services, and address.
  - Bainbridge: create a separate LinkedIn business profile.
- **Logo updates** across all profiles — push the refreshed marks (Woodland + Bainbridge) into Google, Yelp, LinkedIn, Facebook (if active), Better Business Bureau (if applicable).
- **Copy alignment** — make sure positioning lines match across all profiles. Use the canonical signatures from the brand language doc.
- **Posting cadence** (matches the Quarterly Communications Plan):
  - Quarterly educational post → cross-posted to LinkedIn personal, LinkedIn business, GBP post.
  - Quarter-close video update → posted as a LinkedIn article and a GBP video post.
- **Possible automation:** use a transcription pipeline (Whisper or AssemblyAI) on the monthly video updates → first draft of a LinkedIn article → human edit → publish. Worth piloting on one quarter before committing to it as a process.

### Notes
- This is the lowest-priority workstream. Most of it is execution against decisions already made; what matters is doing it consistently, not designing anything new.
- The automation is a nice-to-have. Don't gate the comms cadence on it.

### Open questions
- Who owns the comms calendar internally — one person needs the seat.
- Do we want an outside agency or VA to handle profile maintenance, or is this in-house?
- Are we doing paid LinkedIn or sticking with organic?

### Dependencies
- Team photography pass (drives Google, Yelp, LinkedIn refresh).
- Final logo + canonical positioning copy frozen.
- Comms calendar owner named.

### Recommended next action
Defer until the production work above is in flight. When it's time to start, claim and audit each profile in one sitting, then refresh all of them in a single content pass so the brand lands everywhere on the same day.

## Open questions to resolve before kickoff

1. **Bainbridge identity scope** — is Bainbridge a sister brand with its own mark + palette, or a sub-mark sharing Woodland's full system? This decision unblocks business cards, email signatures, and any Bainbridge-specific notepad/stationery.
2. **Vendor strategy** — one local print shop for cards/letterhead/notepads/notes (consistent paper batch), or specialty vendors per item (letterpress shop for cards/notes, pad shop for notepads, large-format vendor for stair piece)? Recommendation: one specialty letterpress shop for cards + thank-yous; a pad shop for notepads; a sign shop for the office sign and acrylic; an archival print lab for the stair piece.
3. **Office wall surveys** — none of the in-office items can be quoted accurately without measurements and photos of the actual walls (signing room, awards pillars, stairwell).
4. **Photography pass** — multiple downstream items (LinkedIn refresh, GBP photos, possibly the stair-wall imagery) depend on a 2–3 hour studio/office shoot. Schedule this before Phase 4.

## Cross-task dependencies

- **Bainbridge identity locked** → unblocks #1 (cards), #5 (signatures), and any Bainbridge variant of #4 (notepads).
- **Office wall surveys** → unblock #6 (sign), #7 (awards), #8 (stair).
- **Logo + positioning frozen** → unblocks #2 (letterhead), #5 (signatures), and #9 (profile refresh).
- **Photography pass** → unblocks #9 fully and may inform #8.

## Recommended phasing

- **Phase 3a (now):** Email signatures (#5), business cards (#1), letterhead + Qualia template (#2). Low vendor friction, daily impact, finishes the touchpoint system.
- **Phase 3b:** Notepads (#4) and thank-you notes (#3). Same paper family as cards — quote together.
- **Phase 3c:** Acrylic awards displays (#7). Same vendor as the Phase 2 hallway pair.
- **Phase 3d:** Office signing-room sign (#6) and stair wall (#8). Both vendor- and survey-dependent; treat as the office's permanent installation pass.
- **Phase 4:** Public profile refresh (#9). Separate workstream after the production above.
